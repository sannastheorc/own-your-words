# Research basis

SEEDSED. Gathered 2026-11-02 from abstracts and summaries, so no effect sizes are claimed as checked.

- **People with intellectual disability can learn to read with explicit, sustained teaching.** A randomized study of 28 children with moderate ID (Allor et al.) and a 2019 meta-analysis (Sermier Dessemontet et al.) support systematic phonics. [Allor](https://www.smu.edu/-/media/Site/Simmons/Research/IEBE/Teaching-Students-with-Moderate-Intellectual-Disabilities-to-Read.pdf) · [Linder et al. 2023, citing the meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feduc.2023.1190838)
- **Direct evidence for adults with ID is thin.** The READ-IT trial's plan is published, but no results were found. [Protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8783183/)
- **Spelling from memory:** Cover, Copy, Compare and retrieval practice have support, mostly from small studies of children. [CCC review](https://uqu.edu.sa/en/jep/152115) · [Retrieval](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9330-6/fulltext.html)
- **Vocabulary:** teaching words helped comprehension, more for students with reading difficulties, but definitions alone tend to do little; hence meaning, gaps and his own sentences together. [Meta-analysis summary](https://dev.taleafrica.com/2021/04/01/the-impact-of-vocabulary-instruction-on-passage-level-comprehension-of-school-age-children-a-meta-analysis/) · [IRRC](https://irrc.education.uiowa.edu/node/2196)
- **Games:** the learning appears to come from the teaching, not game features, so Block Drop is a reward and the other games are the practice itself. [Review](https://frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00132/text)
- **Not evidence-tested:** this app's exact routine, games and thresholds. They're reasoned choices, and a reading specialist should review them.
